# A converse obstruction and proofs of Paul D. Hanna's self-substitution conjectures after Li–Zhong–Zhou

Haobo Ma (ChronoAI Pte Ltd, Singapore), followed by Wenlin Zhang
(National University of Singapore; ChronoAI Pte Ltd, Singapore).

## Results and coverage

The note gives alternative proofs of the conditional integrality conjecture in the unpaginated
comment of [OEIS A163921](https://oeis.org/A163921), revision #2 of
30 March 2012, including existence and uniqueness over the rationals.
The organizing weighted theorem uses prime-power congruences; a corollary
allows two independently chosen integral logarithmic exponentials.
Geometric dilations cover A157675 and A159593, and the shifted theorem
proves A156907 and A156908 for every integer base, including nonpositive bases.
Li, Zhong and Zhou's polynomial theorem (arXiv:2611.16283v1,
12 November 2026, Theorem 1.2) implies the signed weighted theorem by the
Euler-product reduction given in the note. The direct local proof is an
alternative, not a claim of priority. That source explicitly treats A163138;
the reduction here identifies its consequences for the six entries above.
The square-exponential scalar ingredient is also credited to Bala's
A155200 comment of 25 December 2019, following the positive-base
power-time-change results of Jaidee, Moss and Ward.

The sparse theorem proves integrality and uniqueness for A179471 and all
integer bases, including an Euler-product proof using the general identity
recorded by Manyama in A266964 on 14 November 2017.
It also corrects the missing factor `x` in that entry's
displayed logarithm. Its stated asymptotic constant is not covered.
An infinite family proves that an integral fixed point need not have an
integral exponential input. This converse was not asserted in the source.

The PDF links the two pinned Lean files and states their exact declaration
scopes: the conditional existence/integrality theorem, its base-two
specialization, and the geometric-input lemma. Uniqueness and the weighted,
two-input, shifted, sparse, and converse statements have written proofs.

## Build

With Tectonic 0.17.0:

```sh
tectonic note.tex
```

Alternatively, with a standard TeX installation:

```sh
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error note.tex
pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error note.tex
```

The bibliography is embedded in `note.tex`; BibTeX is unnecessary. Required
packages are `geometry`, `amsmath`, `amssymb`, `amsthm`, `booktabs`, `array`,
`fontenc`, `lmodern`, and `hyperref`.

## Exact finite checks

Python 3 and its standard library suffice:

```sh
python3 verify.py > verification.json
```

The checker compares rational coefficient recursion with formal iteration.
It covers degree 16 for the unshifted and sparse series, and degree 15 for
the normalized shifted series (degree 16 for the shifted series).
All six supplied OEIS coefficient lists are reproduced. Additional checks
cover Catalan coefficients, signed weights from integral Euler products,
sparse bases -2 through 3, and the converse obstructions for primes 2 and 3.
Prime-power congruences are checked for primes at most 31, indices 1 through
40, and bases -3 through 4. The exact coefficients and finite bounds appear
in `verification.json`; the all-degree conclusions follow from the written
proofs, not from finite tests.

For the included checker, the expected SHA-256 of `verification.json` is
`eaacff7aa276f706956ee9a4808c7c4521618f58f5feaf0c0f882a07c27a7ec7`.
The output is regenerated by the command above.
